## Introduction (excerpt, first three paragraphs)

Maize yields in Malawi have stagnated for two decades while fertilizer use
has doubled. This paper shows why the two facts are not a contradiction:
subsidised fertilizer raised yields where it reached the right farmers, and
the 2019 voucher reform is what made the difference.

We study the reform using plot-level panel data on 12,400 farms spanning
2016–2022. Comparing districts that digitised vouchers early against those
that digitised late, yields rose by 310 kilograms per hectare — an 18 per
cent gain over the pre-reform mean — with the entire effect concentrated
among farmers who previously received no voucher at all.

These results matter for a live policy question. Malawi spends 3 per cent of
GDP on input subsidies, and the recurring debate is whether to cut the
programme or fix its targeting. Our estimates imply that better targeting
delivers more than half the yield gain that doubling the budget would, at
roughly one-tenth of the cost.

## Results (excerpt, opening)

Table 2 reports our main estimates. Digitised-voucher districts gained 310
kilograms per hectare (SE 74) relative to comparison districts, an 18 per
cent increase over the pre-reform mean of 1,720. The estimate moves little as
controls are added (columns 1–3), and the event-study in Figure 2 shows flat
pre-trends for four seasons before the reform.
