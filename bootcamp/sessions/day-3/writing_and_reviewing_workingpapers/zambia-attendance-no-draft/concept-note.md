# Concept note — FSMP attendance evaluation (fictional)

## Research question
Does providing a free daily school meal increase primary school attendance,
and for whom?

## Context
Zambia launched the Free School Meals Programme in January 2022 in 46 of 92
districts (phase 1), one hot meal per school day for all pupils in
participating primary schools. Phase-1 districts were selected by the
Ministry, prioritising poorer districts. National scale-up is on the
Ministry's agenda for next year; no Zambian evidence exists to inform it.

## Design
Two-period difference-in-differences on the EMIS school-by-month panel
(1,840 schools, Jan 2021 – Dec 2023): treated vs. comparison districts,
before vs. after January 2022. School and month fixed effects; SEs clustered
at the district level (92 clusters). Because rollout prioritised poorer
districts, baseline imbalance in rural share and assets must be addressed
head-on (controls, district trends, pre-trend evidence) and claims phrased
with care.

## What we found (analysis complete — see outputs/)
- Attendance rose by 4.2 pp in the preferred specification (Table 2 col 2,
  SE 1.1); estimates range 3.9–4.5 pp across specifications. Comparison-group
  baseline: 78.3%.
- Heterogeneity (Table 3): girls +5.6 pp vs boys +2.9; rural +5.1 vs urban
  +2.4.
- Baseline balance (Table 1): attendance and enrollment balanced; rural share
  and asset index imbalanced (by design of the rollout).

## Intended claims (to be refined during writing)
At-scale evidence that school meals raise attendance in Zambia, largest for
girls and rural schools; relevant for the phase-2 targeting decision. No
cost data collected — no cost-effectiveness claims.
