## What this guide is
The policy brief: who the paper is for, and how far the recommendations may
go. Used when drafting the abstract, introduction, and conclusion, and when
checking those sections later.

## When drafting
Name the decision the finding bears on. Translate magnitudes into units that
reader uses. Do not recommend beyond the population and the evidence.

## When commenting
Check that a policy reader can see the answer and that no recommendation
outruns the results. Leave identification and sentence-level style alone.

## Mandate
The audience is a policy counterpart, an operations lead, or a country
economist deciding whether this evidence changes what they do. Relevance must
be real, recommendations earned, and the answer extractable without the
appendix.

## Scope guard
Do not cover: identification mechanics, inference, or numbers integrity
(Methods guide); grammar and sentence-level style (Writing guide); anything
listed in the profile's Non-negotiables.

## Priorities (ranked)
<!-- setup replaces with personalized ranking from the profile's Audience
     section. Stock default: -->
1. The so-what: can the primary reader (see profile Audience) state, after the
   abstract alone, what was found and why it matters to them?
2. Earned recommendations: every policy implication traces to a specific
   result; no recommendation exceeds the setting, population, or magnitude of
   the evidence. Restraint is a feature — flag overreach, praise precision.
3. Magnitudes in usable units: effects translated into terms the audience
   budgets in (cost per beneficiary, share of the gap closed, comparison to a
   known intervention) — not only coefficients and standard deviations.
4. Context sufficiency: enough institutional background that a reader outside
   the country/sector can follow — and no more than that.
5. External validity honesty: where the findings plausibly travel, where they
   don't, stated rather than implied.
6. Competing evidence: the paper positions itself against what the audience
   already believes or uses, not only against the academic literature.

## Comment format
**Quote** the passage → **Concern**: what the target reader would misread,
miss, or reject — name the reader from the profile Audience section →
**Suggestion**: concrete and proportionate → **Severity**: P0 blocks
credibility with this audience / P1 weakens the paper / P2 polish. Never edit
the manuscript.
