The Big Number
58%
of Claude use inside companies
goes through personal accounts, not the company licence

Cyberhaven watches how data moves in and out of AI tools on the work laptops of the firms that use its software. In its 2026 report, 58.2% of Claude use happened through personal accounts. For ChatGPT it was 32.3%, for Gemini 24.9%. Across all tools, about two in five interactions with an AI tool included something the firm classified as sensitive.

That is not surprising. The personal app and the enterprise app look identical, the personal one is cheaper, and it was probably installed first. Impact Analytics’ October 22 training on Claude Enterprise was about why the two are not the same product underneath, and what to do about it now that most of us have both.

Source: Cyberhaven 2026 AI Adoption & Risk Report (February 2026); the per-tool figures are in the accompanying post. Vendor data from firms that bought monitoring software, so probably on the low side.

In Case You Missed It

Understand Claude Enterprise and Other WB AI Tools

A quick recap of Impact Analytics’ October 22 training on Claude Enterprise and the other AI tools at the Bank (DECDI only). The Bank now gives most of us Claude Enterprise, GitHub Copilot Enterprise and mAI, and many of us also pay for a personal Claude or ChatGPT. Same names everywhere, different rules underneath.

1.  An interface is not a model. Claude Code, Copilot and mAI are software that packages your request. Claude Opus or GPT-5.5 is the model that answers it. Several interfaces use the same models. What matters for privacy is neither of them. It is where the model runs, and that is set by the licence.
2.  Same app, different licence, different rules. Claude Enterprise runs on servers Anthropic cannot look into and is approved for Strictly Confidential files. A personal licence runs the same models on servers where your conversations can be kept and used. It is approved for Public files only, even with model training switched off.
3.  That is why Enterprise feels expensive. Enterprise credits are priced at roughly what it costs to run the model. Personal plans are subsidised because Anthropic gets your data in return. Twenty dollars goes a lot further on a personal plan. The gap is the price of privacy, not a markup.
4.  Pick the tool by the data. Identified or confidential data (PII scans, cleaning raw survey data, matching on names): Claude Enterprise. Writing or fixing code, with a codebook and made-up data instead of the real thing: GitHub Copilot, the default for coding. Public data, generic code, learning a package: a personal licence is fine, with the safety settings from the training slides. When in doubt, Enterprise.
5.  Know which account you are in. In Claude Code, type /status. In Claude Web or Desktop, look for the WB logo and your email at the bottom of the sidebar. The VS Code extension follows whichever account Claude Web is signed into. After switching, start a new session. Never continue a conversation that began under the other account.

Try This Today  ·  10 minutes

Find out which account you are actually using, and make the personal one safer. No project files needed.

1. Check Claude in VS Code. Open the Claude panel and type /status. If it shows your WB email, you are on Enterprise. If it shows a personal address, that is the account every Claude task in VS Code has been going to.

2. Check Claude Web and Desktop. Open the sidebar and look at the bottom. WB logo and WB email means Enterprise. Anything else is personal. If you have both, note which browser or app is signed into which.

3. Switch Claude Code from Auto to Manual. Auto approves costly actions on its own and can read files outside your workspace. Manual asks first. The training recommends Manual on every account, and insists on it for personal ones.

4. Sort one task from this week. Take something you are about to hand to an AI tool and ask: what will the tool see? Real data, or a codebook? Unpublished numbers, or public text? Then match it to point 4 above. If the honest answer is “the real data”, it goes to Enterprise, whatever it costs.

Have more than 10 minutes? Set up two Claude Code profiles, one for Enterprise and one for personal, each with its own login and settings. Then you pick the account by opening the profile and never log out again. Steps are in Appendix B2–B3 of the training slides. Or try Workflow B on one dataset: ask Enterprise for a codebook and fifty made-up rows, strip anything that names a place or a person, and do the coding in Copilot. The Deep Dive walks through it.

Field Notes  ·  beyond the trainings

Ask again before you switch tools

The Claude Enterprise training’s ladder says: start in Copilot, move to Claude when Copilot struggles. But how many tries counts as struggling?

A study asked a model to write code for 1,088 published economics and finance papers. Simply asking again improved the code, with shrinking returns each time. A person pointing out what was wrong improved it more. Field Notes takes the result apart, including what the paper cannot tell you, and turns it into a rule for when to climb a rung.
