The Big Number
17.7% → 46.5%
Share of Black patients getting extra help,
before and after fixing the bias

A health algorithm used on about 200 million patients a year was biased against Black patients. It used health costs to measure health needs. But less is spent on Black patients who are just as sick as White patients. So the algorithm wrongly concluded that Black patients were healthier. Fixing this would have raised the share of Black patients getting extra help from 17.7% to 46.5%.

By standard accuracy checks, cost looked like a good measure. It wasn’t. The same risk exists in our work whenever a measure is wrong more often for some groups. Household spending used to measure welfare is one example. AI-coded survey answers that are less accurate in some languages are another. Before you trust a result, ask: what does this measure really capture, who is it wrong for, and what happens if it is wrong?

Source: Obermeyer et al., Science (2019)

In Case You Missed It

AI Ethics, Governance, and Safe Use

A quick recap of the September 17 session. We already use AI in our work. The question now is how to use it carefully. The main message: ethics is not a box you tick at the end. It matters at every stage of the research.

Bank rules
1.  Know the five risks. The WBG AI Risk Management Framework groups them as: accountability and traceability, validity and accuracy, security and privacy, ethical, and legal.
2.  Check the file first, then pick the tool. Every file has a classification: Public, Official Use Only, Confidential, or Strictly Confidential. Only use tools the Bank has approved for that classification. Also check the data licence, data agreement, or IRB protocol. They may not allow sharing the data with any AI tool, even an approved one. Personal ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini accounts are for public files only, even with training switched off.
Good research practice
3.  You own the output. A named person has to review, and be able to override, what the AI produces for each individual case.
4.  Check the source yourself. “Verified” means you opened the page, citation, or code and read it. A number that looks right may come from Wikipedia, a news story, or a Facebook post.
5.  Following the rules is the minimum. A result can follow every rule and still be wrong. At each stage (design, data collection, processing, analysis, publication), ask three questions about your result: is it correct, who is harmed if it isn’t, and what happens then? The rules are the C in TACTIC, the short checklist below. The other five letters cover the rest.

Try This Today  ·  10 minutes

Pick one task you’re about to give an AI tool. Before you send anything, do these checks.

1. Find the file’s classification. Public, Official Use Only, Confidential, or Strictly Confidential. Most everyday work is Official Use Only. Research data is usually Confidential. If you don’t know the classification, find out before going further.

2. Pick a tool that fits the classification. Official Use Only and Confidential files can go into approved Bank tools such as mAI and M365 Copilot. The current list is on AI@WBG. Personal ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini accounts are for public files only, and only if you meet the four conditions in the Acceptable Use of AI Addendum. Switching off training doesn’t change this.

3. If the data is confidential or under a restrictive licence, don’t give the AI the real data. This applies whether or not the data identifies people. Instead, share the codebook and a small made-up dataset with the same structure. Keep the real data on your side. Also check that your data agreement and IRB protocol allow the data to be shared with an AI tool.

4. Run TACTIC:
  Transparency: Will you say where AI helped?
  Accountability: Have you checked the claims yourself?
  Context: Have you used what you know about the country, the institution, and the people?
  Take ownership: Is a person still making the decision?
  Impact: Who is harmed if this is wrong?
  Compliance: Right tool, right classification, data agreement respected?

If every answer is yes, go ahead. If you’re unsure, write to ai@worldbankgroup.org. TACTIC comes from the Using AI Responsibly@WBG course.

Have more than 10 minutes? Run TACTIC on something AI helped with this month, like a citation, a table, or some cleaning code. Open the source behind one number and check it.

What GitHub Copilot’s enterprise terms cover

With a Business or Enterprise plan, GitHub doesn’t use your prompts to train its models. But your prompts are still sent to the company that runs the model. How long they’re kept depends on where you use Copilot, which model you choose, and your licence. About 12 minutes. If this article and Bank guidance disagree, follow the Bank.
