The Big Number
17.7% → 46.5%
Black patients flagged for extra care,
before and after the measure of need was fixed

A health algorithm applied to about 200 million people a year predicted healthcare cost, and treated that cost as a measure of health need. Less money has historically been spent treating Black patients at the same level of illness, so the score tracked who could get care. Equally sick Black patients came out looking healthier. Fixing the measure would have raised the share of Black patients flagged for extra care from 17.7% to 46.5%. A result can fit the data and still be wrong for the people it is about. The same risk sits in our own work when a convenient measure stands in for need, welfare, or a coded answer. The check has to ask who is left out, and how that changes the result.

Source: Obermeyer et al., Science (2019)

In Case You Missed It

AI Ethics, Governance, and Safe Use

Quick recap of the September 17 session. The hour was about using AI so the work stays accurate, fair, secure, and easy to stand behind. For World Bank work, follow the Bank’s rules.

1.  The WBG AI Risk Management Framework groups what can go wrong into five risks: accountability and traceability, validity and accuracy, security and privacy, ethical, and legal.
2.  You own the output. A named person has to be able to review and correct an individual case. An overall accuracy score describes the system. It leaves that person’s case unchanged.
3.  Check the original. “Verified” means you opened the page, the citation, or the code and read it. A number that looks right can rest on Wikipedia, a news article, or a Facebook post. The sources can change with the language of the question.
4.  Start with the label on the information, then choose the tool. An approved tool still needs a separate yes for that data. A personal ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini account stays limited to public information, even with training switched off.
5.  Permission is the starting point. At each stage of the work (design, collection, processing, analysis, publication) ask two questions: is it right, and who is harmed if it is wrong? The short habit for that check is TACTIC, below.

Try This Today  ·  10 minutes

Take one task you are about to give an AI tool. Run the session’s two checks before you send anything: the data label, then TACTIC. Use the filename and what you already know. Leave the file where it is.

1. Name the label. Public, Official Use Only, Confidential, or Strictly Confidential. Most daily work is Official Use Only. Research microdata is Confidential. If you cannot name the label, stop and label the file.

2. Choose the tool from the label. Official Use Only and Confidential go to approved World Bank tools: mAI and M365 Copilot. Gemini enterprise is cleared for World Bank content, including Strictly Confidential. Personal ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini take public information only, and only when all four conditions in the Acceptable Use of AI Addendum hold. The rule stays the same if training is switched off.

3. If the task is code on identified survey data, send the codebook and a made-up extract with the same structure. Keep the real rows and the full file on your side. The data agreement is a separate permission. The IRB list names people, so the tool needs to be named there too.

4. Run TACTIC on what you are about to send, and on what you plan to keep:
  Transparency: will you say where AI helped in a way that matters?
  Accountability: have you checked the claims, and do you own them?
  Context: have you applied what you know about the institution, the country, and the culture?
  Take ownership: is a person still in charge of the decision?
  Impact: who is harmed if this is wrong?
  Compliance: approved tool, right label, data agreement respected?

Send it when every letter passes. If you are unsure, write to ai@worldbankgroup.org.

Have more than 10 minutes? Repeat TACTIC for one output you already kept this month, such as a citation, a table, or a block of cleaning code. Open the source behind one number. This issue’s Deep Dive explains what GitHub publishes about text you send through enterprise Copilot.
