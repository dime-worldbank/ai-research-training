# Abstract

We evaluate a cash plus program in rural Kenya that combines unconditional cash transfers with
nutrition coaching for households with children under five. Using a cluster-randomized trial
across 60 villages and 1,200 households, we find that the program increases household
consumption per capita by 12 percent and reduces food insecurity by 9 percentage points one
year after the first transfer. Effects on child height-for-age are positive but imprecise.
Impacts are larger for female-headed households and for households farther from markets, which
suggests that the coaching component matters most where access to diverse foods is limited.
